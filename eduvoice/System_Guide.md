**CHAPTER I**

**INTRODUCTION **

**BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY**

The Philippines is one of the most disaster-prone countries in the world because of its location along the Pacific Ring of Fire, where several active tectonic plates converge. This geographical setting makes the country highly vulnerable to earthquakes, which can occur without warning and pose significant risks to lives, property, and infrastructure. According to the Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology (PHIVOLCS), the country experiences an average of about 20 recorded earthquakes each day, although most are too weak to be felt (Department of Education [DepEd], 2022). Since earthquakes cannot be predicted, disaster preparedness and effective communication are essential in reducing risks and ensuring public safety.

Schools are among the institutions that must maintain a high level of disaster preparedness because they accommodate large numbers of students, teachers, and staff every day. Student volunteer organizations, such as the Red Cross Youth (RCY) and the Boy Scouts, play an important role in supporting disaster risk reduction activities and assisting during emergencies. Likewise, the Department of Science and Technology (DOST) and PHIVOLCS continue to encourage science-based disaster preparedness initiatives to improve resilience and emergency response in schools (DOST & PHIVOLCS, 2025).

One challenge during earthquake emergencies is that not all youth volunteers immediately become aware that an earthquake has occurred, especially when the shaking is weak or mild. Some volunteers may not feel the tremor because they are engaged in school activities, are located in different areas of the campus, or are less sensitive to ground movement. As a result, they may remain unaware of the situation while other volunteers have already begun responding. Delayed awareness can reduce the efficiency of communication and coordination during emergencies, highlighting the need for a system that can quickly notify all concerned volunteers once an earthquake is experienced. Studies have shown that timely communication plays an important role in improving disaster preparedness and emergency response among students and school communities (Haresco & San Jose, 2021).

Although several mobile applications provide earthquake information and emergency notifications, many are intended for the general public and primarily focus on delivering hazard updates or official warnings. Few applications are specifically designed to support structured communication and coordination among school-based youth volunteer organizations during earthquake emergencies. Likewise, commonly used messaging applications do not provide an organized emergency reporting system that enables volunteer leaders to monitor the status of their members and coordinate emergency response efficiently. These limitations highlight the need for a communication system specifically designed for school-based youth volunteers.

To address this gap, this study proposes the development of Ready Alert, a mobile application specifically designed to improve communication and coordination among selected youth volunteers at Polomolok National High School during earthquake emergencies. The application utilizes a hierarchical communication structure consisting of one Host, designated Youth Volunteer Leaders, and their assigned Members. It also incorporates a Tri-Alarm System, which enables designated volunteer leaders to immediately notify the Host and their assigned members when an earthquake is experienced. Through this structured communication process, volunteers who may not have personally felt a mild earthquake can be promptly informed, allowing them to perform appropriate safety procedures and remain updated about the emergency situation. In addition, the system enables volunteer leaders and the Host to monitor the emergency status of volunteer groups, improving communication and coordination throughout the organization.

Ultimately, this study aims to strengthen earthquake preparedness among selected youth volunteers at Polomolok National High School by providing a dedicated communication platform for earthquake emergencies. Through timely notifications, organized emergency status reporting, and a structured communication hierarchy, Ready Alert seeks to enhance coordination among the Host, Youth Volunteer Leaders, and Members, enabling volunteers to respond more efficiently during earthquake emergencies and contributing to a safer and more organized school-based disaster response.

‎**SCOPE AND DELIMITATION **

This study aims to develop a mobile application called Ready Alert, designed to improve emergency preparedness during earthquake emergencies. The application features a Tri-Alarm System that uses three alert levels—Green, Yellow, and Red—to notify members and youth volunteers about the current emergency situation and provide timely information during an earthquake.

The study focuses exclusively on earthquake emergencies. It does not cover other types of disasters, such as fires, floods, or other natural and man-made hazards. In addition, the application requires an internet connection to function and does not support offline operation, automatic emergency detection, or real-time integration with government emergency response agencies.

The participants of this study are limited to selected youth volunteers at Polomolok National High School, particularly members of youth organizations such as the Red Cross Youth (RCY) and the Boy Scouts.

Furthermore, the development, implementation, and evaluation of the Ready Alert application will be conducted exclusively at Polomolok National High School. The findings of this study are therefore limited to the selected participants and the research setting.

In this study, an earthquake refers to one of the emergency situations addressed by the Ready Alert application, where users receive alerts, preparedness guidelines, and evacuation information. ‎

**SCIENTIFIC BASIS**

The development of Ready Alert is based on the scientific principles of disaster risk reduction, emergency preparedness, emergency warning systems, and mobile information and communication technology (ICT). Disaster preparedness is a key component of disaster risk reduction because it helps individuals and communities develop the capacity to anticipate, respond to, and recover from emergencies. According to the United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction (UNDRR, 2022), people-centered early warning systems provide timely, reliable, and actionable information that enables individuals to take appropriate protective actions, thereby reducing disaster risks and improving community resilience. This principle supports the development of Ready Alert as a mobile application that facilitates the rapid dissemination of earthquake alerts among youth volunteers.

Emergency warning systems are scientifically recognized as effective tools for improving emergency response. Timely warnings reduce response time and allow individuals to make informed decisions before a hazardous event causes greater harm. In the Philippines, the Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology (PHIVOLCS) emphasizes that although earthquakes cannot be predicted, preparedness and immediate dissemination of reliable information are essential in minimizing their impacts (PHIVOLCS, 2024). These principles support the Tri-Alarm System of Ready Alert, which enables Youth Volunteer Leaders to immediately notify the Host and their assigned Members after an earthquake is experienced, promoting faster communication and coordination.

The development of Ready Alert is also supported by advances in mobile information and communication technology. Smartphones equipped with internet connectivity and push notification services allow information to be transmitted quickly and efficiently among users. Real-time mobile communication improves coordination by enabling users to exchange information immediately during emergencies. These scientific principles provide the technological foundation for Ready Alert, allowing the Host, Youth Volunteer Leaders, and Members to communicate rapidly during earthquake emergencies.

Furthermore, studies on mobile emergency applications have shown that users are more likely to adopt emergency communication technologies when they are perceived as useful, accessible, and easy to use. Mobile-based emergency systems improve communication efficiency and support organized emergency response by enabling users to receive timely notifications and coordinate their actions effectively. These findings support the feasibility of developing Ready Alert as a mobile-based earthquake communication and preparedness application for selected youth volunteers at Polomolok National High School.

**THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK**

This study is anchored on three theories that explain the effectiveness and usability of the Ready Alert mobile application for earthquake communication among youth volunteers.

The first theory is the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) developed by Fred D. Davis (1989). TAM explains that users are more likely to accept and use a technology if they perceive it as useful and easy to use. In this study, TAM serves as the basis for evaluating whether the Ready Alert application is user-friendly and effective in helping the host, leaders, and members communicate during earthquake emergencies.

The second theory is the Protection Motivation Theory (PMT) proposed by R. W. Rogers (1975). PMT states that individuals are more likely to take protective actions when they recognize a threat and believe they can effectively respond to it. In the context of this study, youth volunteers are expected to respond appropriately to earthquake alerts when they understand the seriousness of the situation and trust the application's alert system.

The third theory is the Media Richness Theory (MRT) developed by Richard L. Daft and Robert H. Lengel (1986). This theory suggests that communication becomes more effective when information is delivered through rich media that provide immediate feedback and clear messages. The READY ALERT application uses color-coded signals and real-time notifications to reduce confusion and improve communication among the host, leaders, and members during earthquake emergencies.

These three theories collectively support the development and evaluation of the READY ALERT system. Technology Acceptance Model explains user acceptance, Protection Motivation Theory explains users' emergency response behavior, and Media Richness Theory explains the effectiveness of the application's communication features. Together, they provide the theoretical foundation for assessing the usability, communication efficiency, and emergency response capability of the proposed system.
